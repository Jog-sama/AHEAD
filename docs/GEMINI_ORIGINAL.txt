1. Deep Dive: How "FretSight" Actually Works
When teaching yourself fingerstyle or barre chords (like an F major or B minor transition), two things constantly go wrong: micro-positioning errors (your finger is slightly too far back from the fret wire, causing a buzz, or your ring finger collapses and mutes the adjacent string) and transition latency (your fingers freeze for 800ms moving between shapes). Audio-only apps like Yousician only know which pitch rang out—they have no clue which finger you used or why a note clunked.

Here is the exact 4-layer architecture for FretSight:

Layer 1: The Physical Robot Gimbal & Tactile Metronome (<50ms Local Loop)

Hardware: The exact same 2-servo pan-tilt turret + 1080p webcam + USB/Directional Mic + RGB LED strip (or a 3rd micro-servo acting as a physical tapping metronome pendulum) wired to an Arduino over USB.

Active Fretboard Tracking: Stick two tiny high-contrast markers (or AprilTags) on the headstock and body end of your guitar neck—or simply track the bounding box of your fretting hand with MediaPipe Hands (21 3D keypoints). When you shift your sitting posture while playing, the pan-tilt servos smoothly rotate the camera so the fretboard stays dead-center in the frame.

Layer 2: Spatial-Acoustic Sensor Fusion (Vision + Audio)

Homography Fret Mapping: Using OpenCV perspective warp (cv2.getPerspectiveTransform), map the 4 corners of the guitar neck into a flat 2D grid of 6 strings × 12 frets. Every frame, check which (string, fret) cell your index, middle, ring, and pinky fingertips (landmarks 8, 12, 16, 20) occupy.

Real-Time FFT Audio Check: Simultaneously, run librosa or aubio on the audio stream. To beat background noise in the judging room, clip a cheap $10 piezo contact mic (or run an acoustic pickup/clip-on mic right onto the soundboard) so the room noise is completely rejected. Detect the fundamental frequencies and high-frequency harmonic "buzz" artifacts.

Layer 3: The Thinking Machines Tinker Hook ($500/Person Credits)

Why it wins the Tinker bounty: Thinking Machines' thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Small natively accepts both image and audio inputs via tml-renderers.
